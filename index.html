The integration advantage

I work at the interfaces where complexity tends to live.

For more than two decades, my work has crossed technology and business, strategy and execution, growth and control, people and systems.

I tend to be most useful when a business has outgrown the way it operates — when growth, technology, acquisitions or new markets have moved faster than the organisation around them. My role is to understand the system, find the real problem behind the symptoms, design what needs to change, align people around it, and help make it work.

That pattern has followed me from engineering and industrial operations to post-IPO scale leadership, deep-tech venture building, technology commercialisation, acquisitions and enterprise transformation.

Thinking

Scale Without Breaking

A practical point of view on how complex, technology-enabled companies grow without allowing organisational complexity to outrun execution.

Growth creates organisational debt

The operating model often breaks before the business realises it has outgrown it.

Technology is not transformation

Technology changes outcomes only when roles, processes, decisions and incentives change with it.

KPIs as decision infrastructure

Performance measures should improve decisions, not merely create more reporting.

From founder speed to scalable autonomy

The goal is not bureaucracy. It is enough architecture for talented people to move faster.

From deal thesis to operating reality

The hard part of M&A begins when acquired capability has to become part of a working organisation.

Deep-tech commercialisation is translation

Technical possibility matters only when it becomes commercially legible and operationally executable.
